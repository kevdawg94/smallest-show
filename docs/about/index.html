About the project

No one keeps a complete count of America's independent cinemas

No federal program supports them as a class, so no public body has reason to count them. Smallest Show is counting them, house by house, and keeping a dated record of what they show, starting with the 1,818 the widget reads.

Smallest Show is an independent project, not affiliated with any exhibitor, distributor or ticketing company.

What goes unrecorded

Independent cinemas book their own programs, and those bookings are how films outside the studio release calendar reach American screens: foreign-language releases, documentaries, first features, restorations, repertory on film.

A schedule is public on the theater's own site until the show starts, and then the next week's replaces it. No public archive keeps them. So there is no public answer to how many cinemas a restoration played, whether repertory is growing, or which films played only at independents.

No national record

“There is no single entity that maintains a complete dataset of all independent art house exhibitors.”

Art House Convergence, 2025 Landscape Survey1

We made the last count ourselves. County by county, we check every cinema a directory, a map or a listing names: whether it is open, who owns it and whether its schedule can be read. As of November 10, 2026, we've found 2,717 open independent cinemas. The widget reads 1,818 of them, or 67%. The rest are not read yet, for these reasons:

  • 1,818in the widget
  • 695post their times where the widget can't read them yet
  • 204seasonal, closed part of the year

Counts as of November 10, 2026. The count also set aside more than 2,300 listed cinemas found closed and about 1,700 run by chains.

What counts as independent. Independent describes who owns a cinema, not what it shows, so the count includes local first-run cinemas and drive-ins as well as art houses. A circuit is a chain if investors own it (a public company, private equity, a conglomerate or another exhibitor), if it runs theaters in 10 or more states, or if it runs more than 50. A circuit of 50 or fewer owned by a family, its founder, local owners, its employees or a nonprofit counts cinema by cinema. An art house counts as independent even when a chain runs it.

Elsewhere, cinemas report what they show

Several countries require every cinema to report what it shows, and several pay cinemas for what they program. Between them, they know where their art houses are and what they play.

  • France

    Every cinema reports each screening's admissions and receipts to the national film agency, the CNC, every week, by law.5 In 2026 it classified 1,357 cinemas and travelling circuits Art et Essai and paid them about €19.6 million, scaled to the share of their screenings that went to films the CNC recommends.6

  • Germany

    The Film Act requires every cinema that sells tickets to report admissions and revenue for each film on each screen to the national film board every month.7 A federal premium, Liebling Kino, pays cinemas for what they choose to show: its first round paid €6.7 million to 464 of them.8

  • United Kingdom

    The BFI's Film Audience Network gets £10.8 million of National Lottery money for 2026–29 to build audiences for independent film across the country.9

  • Europe

    Europa Cinemas, the EU-backed network of independent cinemas, counts 1,290 members in 39 countries. To keep its support, each one reports its screenings and admissions film by film.10

  • South Korea and Brazil

    Since 2010, Korea's cinemas have been required by law to report every ticket sold to the national film council's network.11 Brazil's cinemas report every screening each day, and its film agency publishes each screening's attendance as open data.12

  • United States

    No federal program supports cinemas as a class, as France and Germany do, and no public body keeps count of the independents.

In the US, no one keeps count

American support for the arts runs mostly through tax deductions for private gifts. In 2004 the National Endowment for the Arts called itself “less than 1 percent of total arts philanthropy in the U.S.” It added: “No one—not even the NEA—has exact statistics on American cultural institutions.”13 Its whole budget, for every art form in the country, was $207 million in 2026, and the White House's budget for 2027 proposes closing it.14

The one federal program written for cinemas was pandemic relief, the Shuttered Venue Operators Grants, which kept theaters open without asking what they showed.2 Abroad, money leads to a test and the test to a record. In the US there is no test, so no one has a reason to count.

What we do

Smallest Show documents America's independent cinemas: where each one is and, for the 1,818 whose schedules it reads, what they show and how that changes over time. The case for supporting them starts with that record.

Up to five times a day, we read the public schedules of every theater in the widget, openly and under our own name. We keep every read, dated. Together they are a running record of what America's independent cinemas show.

Every cinema the widget doesn't read yet is filed with the reason, so the gap can close one theater at a time. If you run a cinema, or one you go to is missing, write to us at info@smallest.show.
