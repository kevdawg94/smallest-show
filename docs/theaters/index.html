For theaters

If smallest-show appears in your server logs, it is us. This page explains what we read from your website or ticketing platform, how often, and how to ask for a change.

What Smallest Show is

Smallest Show is a free iPhone widget that lists tonight's showtimes at independent cinemas across the US. It is also a count of those cinemas and a dated record of what they show. Every showtime links to the theater's own ticket page, and every theater's name to its website, so the sale happens where it always does. The widget has no ads or accounts and sells nothing. See the widget.

What we read

We read only the public schedule pages and feeds your website or ticketing platform already serves, the listings your customers see. Nothing behind a login, and no browser automation.

How often

We read ticketing platforms up to five times a day, between 9am and 9pm Pacific. We read a theater's own website twice a day, around 6am and 6pm Eastern.

How we read

  • No more than two requests at a time to any one site, each asking for compressed pages, so a read is light.
  • We stop when asked. We don't retry a refused request. We honor 429 responses and Retry-After headers. A site that refuses a read is left alone until its next scheduled read.
  • Every request, scheduled or made by hand, carries this user agent: smallest-show/1.0 (independent cinema showtimes; https://smallest.show/theaters/)

What we publish and keep

Your theater's name, location and website. Each film's title, rating and running time. Each showtime, whether it is sold out and its ticket link. No images, descriptions, prices or seat counts. Only the next seven days are published. We keep a dated copy of each read for our own research. The copies aren't published.

Changes and opting out

To have us read a different page or feed, read less often, correct a listing or leave your theater out, write to us at info@smallest.show. Name the theater and the change, and we'll make it.

Made by Kevin Celustka. Named for The Smallest Show on Earth (1957), in which a young couple inherit a run-down cinema between two railway bridges.

Free, with no accounts, ads or tracking.
